Entry flags under the candles — what each one means
▲P Pocket pivot — an early buy inside a base, or off the 10-day in an uptrend, before the obvious breakout (Morales & Kacher). An up day (close above the prior close) whose volume is at least the largest down-day volume of the prior 10 sessions, closing above the 50-day average, with the day's low right at or just below the 10-day average (at most 1% above it) and the close back above the 10-day.
▲G Gap-up buy point — opens above the prior day's high; the gap from the prior close is at least 0.75× the average true range of the previous 40 sessions; volume is at least 1.5× its 50-day average; and the day's range comes within 5% of the base high. Stop = the gap day's low.
▲B Breakout — the first close above the base high (the highest high of the prior 6 months, set at least 3 weeks earlier) on volume at least 1.5× its 50-day average — the book's 50%-above-average breakout rule.
△ Not a buy — a pocket pivot or gap that breaks one of the book's rules — extended (low more than 1% above the 10-day), closed below the 10-day, below the 50-day, a downtrend (50-day falling, under the 200-day), the lower third of the base, wedging up on lighter volume, a V-shaped drop to the 20-day that never touched the 50-day, a wide and loose base (deeper than 50% or weekly swings of 10%+), or a gap extended more than 5% above the base high or still inside the base. Shown for the last 60 sessions only.
The flags are judged on SIMPLE moving averages, as in the book's charts: the 10-day (location, extended) and 50-day (above it), plus the 20-day (V-shape) and 200-day (downtrend). Every chart shows the 10- and 50-day lines — dotted where the page's own lines are EMAs.
Hover a marker to see its numbers, whether the day closed in the upper half of its range (the book calls a mid-range close weak) and whether trading was quiet before it. The thresholds freeze a discretionary book (Trade Like an O'Neil Disciple, ch. 5) into rules: reading aids, not tested signals. On NSE only the breakout-day versions are measured — gap-ups helped (42.3% vs 34.5% reached +20% in 60 days); pocket-pivot volume barely did, because 77% of breakouts have it.
PP_* columns — RichRoad's pocket-pivot workflow, shown not applied. For the latest pocket pivot in the last 5 sessions (up day, volume above the biggest down-volume day of the prior 10): PP_recent = where it sat (a bounce off the 10-day EMA, off the 50-day EMA, or extended) and how long ago · PP_vol_x = its volume ÷ that biggest down day · PP_close_pct = close position in the day's range · PP_notes = EMA stack 10>20>50>200, a volume-dry-up day (≤50% of the 50-day average) in the prior 5, and whether the stock spent ≥80% of the last ~5 months below its 200-day (his "no long downtrends" rule) · PP_zone = the PP candle's low to its midpoint — where he buys the pullback rather than the PP day · PP_stop = the candle low −1% · PP_state = today's price against that zone.
⚠️ Backtested 2026-09-21 (983 liquid NSE names, 2011-26): the pocket-pivot volume day adds nothing measurable over the same gates without it (20-session mean +2.8% vs +2.5% over all years; +1.4% vs +1.6% in the 2025+ holdout). The gates themselves (RS ≥ 80, above the 200-DMA, on the 10/50 average, EMA stack) are what carried the return. A tight PP-low stop was hit far more often than it saved money (only ~1 in 3 trades finished up at 20 sessions). Round 2 (2026-09-22) tested the second source's rules — above the 11-week EMA, tight 5-week range with contracted volume, a candle touching the 10 EMA, near a 52-week high, SMA vs EMA — and none lifted returns (the weekly-quality combination was worse: +2.2% vs +2.7%). Exit rules with only an 8% hard stop: 10→50 EMA +5.2% all years but −1.0% in the 2025+ holdout; 10→20 EMA +2.2% / +0.4%; median trade negative in all of them (wins are few and large). Averages: PP_* columns use 10/20/50 EMAs; the ▲P flag and Entry_flag use the book's 10/50 simple averages — the backtest found no difference between them. Trend Template 0/9: every price condition (above the 50/150/200DMA, 50 > 150 > 200, 200DMA rising, ≥30% above the 52-week low, within 25% of the 52-week high). That template is his Stage 2 test; nothing else is stacked on it. RS = MarketSmith RS_Rating ≥0 — his Trend Template criterion 8 ("no less than 70, preferably in the 80s or 90s"). RS_80plus flags SPEC.md Tier 1 (RS ≥ 80): False = passes his rule but not Tier 1 — type true in its filter box to keep only Tier 1 names. How far above: a name stays if it is at most 5% past its pivot or at most 5% above its 52-week high; below either, by any amount, is fine (the Trend Template bounds that). The State column still says EXTENDED — Minervini does not buy those; filter !extended to hide them. CANSLIM view: click the Comp_Rating header to rank by MarketSmith's Composite Rating (a triage number combining EPS, RS, group, SMR and A/D — not a buy signal); every column sorts. Base stage = MarketSmith's count of bases since the move began — shown, not gated (user decision 2026-09-13); 3rd-stage and later bases are late and fail more often, so filter the Base_Stage_Daily column (e.g. 1,2) to apply Minervini's preference. Group rank is shown, not gated, on this run.
Trend: two speeds, as in MarketSmith's own Minervini lists. Confirmed (5m) = 200DMA higher at each of the last five monthly checkpoints (the 5-month template, a confirmed Stage 2). Emerging (1–4m) = 200DMA rising for less than that (the 1-month template): a stock just turning up out of a base, "not confirmed yet". Emerging names are earlier and less proven; the two are grouped, never blended.
States. BREAKOUT = 0–5% past MarketSmith's pivot on expansion (RVOL ≥ 1.5). BUY RANGE = 0–5% past the pivot on quieter volume, still inside his 5% rule; the volume confirmation came on an earlier bar, so check the chart. SETUP = base forming, within 15% below the pivot. Dry_up (today's RVOL < 1) is a flag, not a gate: he reads volume drying up across the base's contractions, not from one day's bar. RETEST = cleared the pivot and is back below it by less than 5%: not a buy until it reclaims the pivot. vs Pivot names the measure on each row: "x% to pivot" for a base below it, "+x% from pivot" once through it. Listed since 2026-09-13 (user rule): every state — EXTENDED (more than 5% past the pivot; he does not chase — shown only while within 5% of the 52-week high), failed breakout (more than 5% back below the pivot), deep base (more than 15% below it) and no pivot. Not listed (counted in the header): only names more than 5% past the pivot AND more than 5% above the 52-week high. Use the State filter box to narrow — e.g. setup,buy,breakout.
Book annotations (Detail chart, sepa_patterns.py — Minervini ch. 10 figures). T1 / T2 / T3 label each contraction with its depth. VCP ✓ / ✗ (top-left of the price pane): ✓ needs all three — each contraction at most 85% as deep as the one before, lower volume in the last contraction than the first, and a last contraction ≤ 10%; ✗ names what failed. Shakeout ▲ — a low that undercuts the prior contraction's low by 0.2–10% and closes back above it within 3 sessions (DECK / MGA figures); hover it for the level and the volume. CHEAT — the 3C / cheat area: a tight pause (5–15 sessions, ≤ 7% range, volume below average) in the middle of the base — 33–60% of the way back up, its top at least 5% below the base high (the upper third is the handle's); its high is the earliest buy point (CRUS / VCI figures). Power play — +100% in ≤ 8 weeks on a big-volume day, then 12–30 sessions sideways ≤ 25% deep on lower volume (TASR / QSII). Dry-up — blue volume bar: a day in the last 10 sessions at ≤ 40% of the 50-day average that is also one of the 3 quietest days of the whole base ("day before breakout, lowest volume"). EPS boxes along the top of the price pane — one per reported quarter, placed at the quarter's end: "EPS +66% · Sales +91%" vs the same quarter a year earlier, from MarketSmith's evaluation page (Quarterly Earnings table); green = EPS ≥ 25% and at least as fast as the quarter before (accelerating), yellow = ≥ 25% but slowing, red = below 25%. EPS_Qs (table) = the last four quarters' EPS % change, oldest first. All are descriptions to read the chart by — not scores, not filters, not validated on NSE.
The base and its contractions (Detail chart). The shaded band is the current base: it starts at the peak of the latest Stage-2 advance, after which the stock pulled back to its 50-day average (or corrected 10% or more): Minervini's "Stage 2, then consolidation" (Trade Like a Stock Market Wizard, ch. 10). Each blue line starts at one contraction's high or low and runs to the right edge, its price tagged in the margin; the green dashed line is MarketSmith's pivot. VCP_footprint is his notation: weeks in the base, deepest / last contraction %, and the number of contractions, e.g. "8W 19/5 3T". FinalT_vol = volume through the last contraction as a multiple of its 50-day average (he wants it below 1). Both are descriptions to read the chart by, not filters.
⚠️ Contradiction: the book treats a very tight last contraction (2–3%) as the ideal pivot; our 5-year NSE breakout study found breakouts from a tight final contraction (8% or less) did worse (−9.1 points, −8.0 out of sample). Read the footprint; do not rank by it.
Entry flags under the candles (Morales & Kacher, Trade Like an O'Neil Disciple, ch. 5):
  • ▲P pocket pivot: an up day on volume at least as large as every down day of the prior 10 sessions, closing above the 50-day, with the day's low right at or just below the 10-day (at most 1% above it) and the close back above it. None of the book's do-not-buy cases may apply: extended, closed below the 10-day, below the 50-day, downtrend, lower third of the base, wedging, V-shape, or wide and loose.
  • ▲G gap-up buy point: a gap of at least 0.75× ATR(40) on at least 1.5× volume, within 5% of the base high. The stop is the gap day's low.
  • ▲B breakout: the first close above a base high at least 3 weeks old, on at least 1.5× volume (the book's 50% above average).
  • Grey outline: a pocket pivot or gap the book says not to buy (last 60 sessions). Hover any marker for its reasons.
Entry_flag = the latest of these in the last 10 sessions (✗ = a do-not-buy one).
⚠️ The tolerances are frozen choices; the book is discretionary. On NSE only the breakout-day versions are measured: gap-ups helped (42.3% vs 34.5% reached +20% in 60 days); pocket-pivot volume barely did, because 77% of breakouts have it.

How to read the four panes.
RPR — relative price performance (1-99). A weighted blend of 63/126/189/252-day price change (2·P3 + P6 + P9 + P12, /5), percentile-ranked across the whole universe on every bar, so the trajectory is visible and not just today's number. The shaded band is 80+, Minervini's "preferably in the 80s or 90s". The orange line is MarketSmith's own RS_Rating for the same stock, stacked out of our daily snapshots — so it starts only when the archive did (late June 2026) and covers the right-hand part of the pane. The two disagree because they are different measurements: ours ranks OUR cache on a 63/126/189/252 weighting, theirs uses their universe and their maths. A persistent gap between the lines is worth a look, not an error. Blue dots mark the RS line (stock ÷ NIFTY 50) at a new 52-week high, red at a new low — a blue dot while price is still inside the base is the signal: leadership showing up before the breakout does.
Trend Template (0-9) — the stand-in for TPR. The 9 published conditions scored per bar: green 9/9, yellow 7-8, red below 7. The line is the score, the band is the verdict. ⚠️ It is not Minervini's TPR: on Markets 360, TPR is the Technical Profile Rank, in his words "a proprietary behavioral analytics model that evaluates a stock's current technical condition against the Leadership Profile of history's greatest winners" — letter-graded and not reproducible from price alone. The Trend Template is the closest published rule to it, so that is what is drawn.
Buy Risk — % above the 50DMA. Green 0-15% (the buy zone), yellow 15-25% (caution), red >25% (do not buy — the probability of a pullback is high), grey below the MA where the extension rule does not apply.
Pressure. Four states, because "under pressure" covers two different warnings. Green = constructive. Amber = the last two completed weeks each undercut the previous week's low, while price is still above its 50DMA — inside a VCP that is often just the handle forming on dry volume, so it is a "read the chart" signal, not an exit. Red = the close is below the 50DMA, so the short-term trend is damaged and the buy-zone rule no longer applies. Solid red = below the 50DMA on ≥1.5× average volume — institutions selling into it, the only unambiguous one.
What this is, and what it is not. These reproduce the published rules of the Minervini Markets 360 panes from our own price cache — they are not Minervini's code, and every threshold is his published number rather than one validated against forward returns here. MonAlert Net is proprietary (a 58-signal engine) and is deliberately not reproduced. RPR is OUR percentile over OUR cache and will disagree with MarketSmith's RS_Rating, which uses their universe and methodology — both are shown on every panel so the divergence is visible instead of hidden.
