Entry flags under the candles — what each one means
▲P Pocket pivot — an early buy inside a base, or off the 10-day in an uptrend, before the obvious breakout (Morales & Kacher). An up day (close above the prior close) whose volume is at least the largest down-day volume of the prior 10 sessions, closing above the 50-day average, with the day's low right at or just below the 10-day average (at most 1% above it) and the close back above the 10-day.
▲G Gap-up buy point — opens above the prior day's high; the gap from the prior close is at least 0.75× the average true range of the previous 40 sessions; volume is at least 1.5× its 50-day average; and the day's range comes within 5% of the base high. Stop = the gap day's low.
▲B Breakout — the first close above the base high (the highest high of the prior 6 months, set at least 3 weeks earlier) on volume at least 1.5× its 50-day average — the book's 50%-above-average breakout rule.
△ Not a buy — a pocket pivot or gap that breaks one of the book's rules — extended (low more than 1% above the 10-day), closed below the 10-day, below the 50-day, a downtrend (50-day falling, under the 200-day), the lower third of the base, wedging up on lighter volume, a V-shaped drop to the 20-day that never touched the 50-day, a wide and loose base (deeper than 50% or weekly swings of 10%+), or a gap extended more than 5% above the base high or still inside the base. Shown for the last 60 sessions only.
The flags are judged on SIMPLE moving averages, as in the book's charts: the 10-day (location, extended) and 50-day (above it), plus the 20-day (V-shape) and 200-day (downtrend). Every chart shows the 10- and 50-day lines — dotted where the page's own lines are EMAs.
Hover a marker to see its numbers, whether the day closed in the upper half of its range (the book calls a mid-range close weak) and whether trading was quiet before it. The thresholds freeze a discretionary book (Trade Like an O'Neil Disciple, ch. 5) into rules: reading aids, not tested signals. On NSE only the breakout-day versions are measured — gap-ups helped (42.3% vs 34.5% reached +20% in 60 days); pocket-pivot volume barely did, because 77% of breakouts have it.

Layer 1 — the universe, on MarketSmith's own numbers (snapshot 2026-11-07):
  • RS_Rating ≥ 80 and EPS_Rating ≥ 70 — SPEC.md's Tier 1 floors.
  • Industry-group rank 1–60 (1 = strongest; MSI's 0 means "group not available").
  • A/D rating A+, A, A−, B+, B or B− — accumulation, not distribution.
  • Base stage 1, 2, 3 in any sub-type: the leading number of MarketSmith's Base_Stage_Daily, so 1b, 2c and 3b all count. Minervini's and O'Neil's edge sits in stage 1–2 bases; a stage-3 base is later in the move and fails more often — the Stage column shows it.
Layer 2 — the flag must be on the last candle (06 Nov 2026, the latest session in our prices). The flags come from entry_signals.py, the same code that draws the markers on every chart page. A stock is listed only when that candle carries at least one book-valid ▲P, ▲G or ▲B; one whose candle carries only a do-not-buy flag is left out and counted in the header. Last candle gives the rule's numbers for that candle — its volume against the largest down day of the prior 10 (or its 50-day average), where its low sat against the 10-day, and where it closed in its range. Earlier flags are not listed; the Detail chart draws them. Grouped by the candle's flag (a breakout outranks a gap, and a gap a pocket pivot, when one candle carries several), then by MSI RS — never scored. A stock with no candle on 06 Nov (no trade, or our prices stopped) is not judged and is named in the header.
SPEC.md Tier 1 also requires price above the 200-day average. This page does not gate on it — the 200-DMA column and the "Tier 1" checkbox show it. A pocket pivot already needs a close above the 50-day, and neither a gap-up nor a breakout is flagged in a downtrend.
Not validated on our data. The flags freeze a discretionary book (Trade Like an O'Neil Disciple, ch. 5) into fixed thresholds. They are reading aids, not tested signals; check the chart before acting, and size from the stop.

How to read the four panes.
RPR — relative price performance (1-99). A weighted blend of 63/126/189/252-day price change (2·P3 + P6 + P9 + P12, /5), percentile-ranked across the whole universe on every bar, so the trajectory is visible and not just today's number. The shaded band is 80+, Minervini's "preferably in the 80s or 90s". The orange line is MarketSmith's own RS_Rating for the same stock, stacked out of our daily snapshots — so it starts only when the archive did (late June 2026) and covers the right-hand part of the pane. The two disagree because they are different measurements: ours ranks OUR cache on a 63/126/189/252 weighting, theirs uses their universe and their maths. A persistent gap between the lines is worth a look, not an error. Blue dots mark the RS line (stock ÷ NIFTY 50) at a new 52-week high, red at a new low — a blue dot while price is still inside the base is the signal: leadership showing up before the breakout does.
Trend Template (0-9) — the stand-in for TPR. The 9 published conditions scored per bar: green 9/9, yellow 7-8, red below 7. The line is the score, the band is the verdict. ⚠️ It is not Minervini's TPR: on Markets 360, TPR is the Technical Profile Rank, in his words "a proprietary behavioral analytics model that evaluates a stock's current technical condition against the Leadership Profile of history's greatest winners" — letter-graded and not reproducible from price alone. The Trend Template is the closest published rule to it, so that is what is drawn.
Buy Risk — % above the 50DMA. Green 0-15% (the buy zone), yellow 15-25% (caution), red >25% (do not buy — the probability of a pullback is high), grey below the MA where the extension rule does not apply.
Pressure. Four states, because "under pressure" covers two different warnings. Green = constructive. Amber = the last two completed weeks each undercut the previous week's low, while price is still above its 50DMA — inside a VCP that is often just the handle forming on dry volume, so it is a "read the chart" signal, not an exit. Red = the close is below the 50DMA, so the short-term trend is damaged and the buy-zone rule no longer applies. Solid red = below the 50DMA on ≥1.5× average volume — institutions selling into it, the only unambiguous one.
