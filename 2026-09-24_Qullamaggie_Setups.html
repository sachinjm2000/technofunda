Qullamaggie — Annotated Setups & Contraction Map

Entry flags under the candles — what each one means
▲P Pocket pivot — an early buy inside a base, or off the 10-day in an uptrend, before the obvious breakout (Morales & Kacher). An up day (close above the prior close) whose volume is at least the largest down-day volume of the prior 10 sessions, closing above the 50-day average, with the day's low right at or just below the 10-day average (at most 1% above it) and the close back above the 10-day.
▲G Gap-up buy point — opens above the prior day's high; the gap from the prior close is at least 0.75× the average true range of the previous 40 sessions; volume is at least 1.5× its 50-day average; and the day's range comes within 5% of the base high. Stop = the gap day's low.
▲B Breakout — the first close above the base high (the highest high of the prior 6 months, set at least 3 weeks earlier) on volume at least 1.5× its 50-day average — the book's 50%-above-average breakout rule.
△ Not a buy — a pocket pivot or gap that breaks one of the book's rules — extended (low more than 1% above the 10-day), closed below the 10-day, below the 50-day, a downtrend (50-day falling, under the 200-day), the lower third of the base, wedging up on lighter volume, a V-shaped drop to the 20-day that never touched the 50-day, a wide and loose base (deeper than 50% or weekly swings of 10%+), or a gap extended more than 5% above the base high or still inside the base. Shown for the last 60 sessions only.
The flags are judged on SIMPLE moving averages, as in the book's charts: the 10-day (location, extended) and 50-day (above it), plus the 20-day (V-shape) and 200-day (downtrend). Every chart shows the 10- and 50-day lines — dotted where the page's own lines are EMAs.
Hover a marker to see its numbers, whether the day closed in the upper half of its range (the book calls a mid-range close weak) and whether trading was quiet before it. The thresholds freeze a discretionary book (Trade Like an O'Neil Disciple, ch. 5) into rules: reading aids, not tested signals. On NSE only the breakout-day versions are measured — gap-ups helped (42.3% vs 34.5% reached +20% in 60 days); pocket-pivot volume barely did, because 77% of breakouts have it.

What the chart marks — and why

  1. Big move — 30–100%+ in the prior 1–3 months. The stairstep that must come first.
  2. Contraction legs (shaded) — each swing-high→swing-low pullback inside the base, labelled with its depth. Want them shrinking left→right: −14% → −8% → −4%.
  3. Holds — bars that dip into the rising 10/21/50 and close back above. Repeated holds = the stock is "surfing" that average.
  4. The grade — "the strongest stocks find support on the 10d, the strong ones the 20d, and the slower ones on the 50d." Each name is graded by the shallowest MA it genuinely respects: ≥85% of closes above it, with at least 2 holds. STRONGEST = 10 EMA · STRONGER = 21 EMA · SLOWER = 50 SMA · CHOPPY = none of them. Cards are sorted by grade before stars, because a 5-star base that respects no MA is the worse trade — the exit rule has nothing to hold on to.
  5. Trail breaks — how many separate times a trailing stop on that MA would have taken you out across the advance and base. This is the HUBS problem made numeric: a stock can have superb relative strength and still be untradeable by this method because the trail gets whipsawed. Low is good.
  6. Tight Range (dashed box) — the terminal contraction, price coiled on the MAs.
  7. Volume Dry Up — last-5 volume vs the 30-day average. Falling supply.
  8. Trigger bar (magenta) — the tight, low-volume, often-inside bar before the breakout. Its high is the pivot. A tight bar means little supply, and it is what lets the stop stay inside the ADR ceiling.
  9. Stale move — Qullamaggie's scanner filter is "1M or 3M or 6M > 30%", but the setup wants the big move in the past 1–3 months. A name clearing only the 6M leg with a flat last month has already digested its move, so it is flagged and kept out of "Actionable".
  10. Choppy — a name respecting none of the 10/21/50 was the worst bucket in both backtest samples (20d win rate 25–31% vs 51–55%). This is the HUBS case from the source: real relative strength, but the MA trail gets whipsawed, so the exit rule cannot survive it. Flagged and excluded from "Actionable", not removed.
  11. Weak industry group — Qullamaggie never screens on industry group, but on NSE data it mattered: group rank >100/197 replicated across two independent backtest samples as materially worse (20d win rate 28–38% vs 51–55%, median −3.3% / −6.6%). Those names are flagged red and excluded from "Actionable", but not removed — the test covers one market regime only. See qullamaggie-factor-backtest-aug2026.
  12. Risk ÷ ADR — the hard gate. Risk must be under 1× ADR, ideally ⅓–½×. Wider than 1× and the entry is rejected, not resized.

On the stop: Qullamaggie's stop is the low of the breakout day, which has not happened yet on a stock still basing. The risk shown uses the trigger-bar low as the proxy for it — that is the level the breakout day opens from. "Wide stop alt." is the conservative version, under the entire tight range. Recompute against the real day-low once the stock actually triggers.
