🎣 Harris Pullback Watchlist

Entry flags under the candles — what each one means
▲P Pocket pivot — an early buy inside a base, or off the 10-day in an uptrend, before the obvious breakout (Morales & Kacher). An up day (close above the prior close) whose volume is at least the largest down-day volume of the prior 10 sessions, closing above the 50-day average, with the day's low right at or just below the 10-day average (at most 1% above it) and the close back above the 10-day.
▲G Gap-up buy point — opens above the prior day's high; the gap from the prior close is at least 0.75× the average true range of the previous 40 sessions; volume is at least 1.5× its 50-day average; and the day's range comes within 5% of the base high. Stop = the gap day's low.
▲B Breakout — the first close above the base high (the highest high of the prior 6 months, set at least 3 weeks earlier) on volume at least 1.5× its 50-day average — the book's 50%-above-average breakout rule.
△ Not a buy — a pocket pivot or gap that breaks one of the book's rules — extended (low more than 1% above the 10-day), closed below the 10-day, below the 50-day, a downtrend (50-day falling, under the 200-day), the lower third of the base, wedging up on lighter volume, a V-shaped drop to the 20-day that never touched the 50-day, a wide and loose base (deeper than 50% or weekly swings of 10%+), or a gap extended more than 5% above the base high or still inside the base. Shown for the last 60 sessions only.
The flags are judged on SIMPLE moving averages, as in the book's charts: the 10-day (location, extended) and 50-day (above it), plus the 20-day (V-shape) and 200-day (downtrend). Every chart shows the 10- and 50-day lines — dotted where the page's own lines are EMAs.
Hover a marker to see its numbers, whether the day closed in the upper half of its range (the book calls a mid-range close weak) and whether trading was quiet before it. The thresholds freeze a discretionary book (Trade Like an O'Neil Disciple, ch. 5) into rules: reading aids, not tested signals. On NSE only the breakout-day versions are measured — gap-ups helped (42.3% vs 34.5% reached +20% in 60 days); pocket-pivot volume barely did, because 77% of breakouts have it.

The six mandatory conditions

Exactly as encoded in upside_reversal.check_conditions(). All six must pass — the candle is rejected the moment any one fails. Conditions 1–5 compare the candle against the previous candle; condition 6 measures it against itself.

1Rejected at support: low pierces a level, close recovers above it
2Closes at/above prior close
3Closes in the upper half of its own range

Why each one

  • 1 · Prior day is a DOWN candle — the decline must actually be in progress. Holds on 5/5 of Harris's own labeled candles.
  • 2 · Opens below prior close — it opens weak. Observed −0.64% to −1.65% on his five.
  • 3 · Low undercuts prior low — the shakeout. Without it there is nothing to reverse. Observed −0.04% to −2.07%.
  • 4 · Closes at or above prior close — >=, not >: LULU 2020-05-04 closed at exactly the prior close (218.69 vs 218.69) and Harris labeled it.
  • 5 · Volume ≥ prior day — equal passes. ⚠️ A user rule, not settled by the evidence: it holds on only 3/5 of his labeled candles (LULU 5/4 and 5/14 had volume 35–39% below the prior day and he labeled them anyway).
  • 6 · Closes ≥80% up its own range — buyers took control into the close. Observed 0.82–1.00, median 0.98.

Which level the reversal fires at

The candle must undercut-and-recover a level, but it does not matter which: 10DMA, 21EMA, 50DMA or the top of a prior consolidation all qualify equally. Where several are listed, the moving averages had converged and one candle cleared the whole stack — the strongest version of the setup. Harris's own ranking: 21EMA is the "primary add zone", the 50DMA "the classic" (first touch = highest conviction), the 10DMA more a stay-in tool than an add zone. He never buys at the 200DMA.

Pullback depth — reported, NOT a filter

⚠️ The old ≥10% off the 52-week high gate was removed on 2026-08-22. Tested against Harris's own marked candles it rejected 5 of 8, including the TDOC candle he personally circles on the video — that one is only 4.2% off its 52-week high. Depth is also not a constant: it scales with which average is being tested (10EMA ~5–7%, 21EMA ~6–14%, 50SMA ~12–15% off the run-up high) and with the stock's own volatility — NIO runs 12–33% for identically-labelled reversals. The WONDA OH figure is still shown as a column, and the 16–18% sweet spot is still worth knowing; it just no longer excludes anything. There is likewise deliberately no duration rule.

Stop loss

Entry on the reversal → SL = the low of that reversal candle. Entry on a pivot breakout → SL = the low of the most recent upside reversal, not the breakout bar's own low.
