O'Neil's rules, per pattern. Cup With Handle — the HANDLE is measured (last touch of the pivot → low), ceiling 8% (his range is 8–12%, tighter in a weak tape), handle 5–20 bars (minimum one week, typically 1–2, rarely past 4). Flat Base — the WHOLE base is measured, ceiling 10% (he allows 10–15%; a flat base is defined by being tight). One ceiling for both would be meaningless: a handle's depth and a base's depth are not the same measurement, and forcing 8% on every pattern returned ZERO Consolidations, Cup-Without-Handles, Double Bottoms and Ascending Bases. Price must still be BELOW the pivot, and RS ≥ 70 (his L: leaders, not laggards).
Red-flag veto — NoInst / LowLiq. Institutional sponsorship is the I in CAN SLIM: only funds can drive a stock 100%+, so a name they do not own has no engine, and low liquidity is the mechanism that prevents them buying it. HighDebt is shown as a demerit, not a veto — O'Neil prefers falling debt/equity but does not disqualify on it alone.
⚠️ The handle is DERIVED, not MarketSmith's. Handle high = the pivot; handle start = the last bar whose high came within 1.5% of it; handle low = the lowest low since. Where MarketSmith's engine drew the base differently the number here will disagree with the chart — check the chart before acting. The upper-half-of-cup test is reported but NOT enforced: it approximates the cup low with a 160-bar minimum and passed every name, so it is not yet a real test.
