Entry flags under the candles — what each one means
▲P Pocket pivot — an early buy inside a base, or off the 10-day in an uptrend, before the obvious breakout (Morales & Kacher). An up day (close above the prior close) whose volume is at least the largest down-day volume of the prior 10 sessions, closing above the 50-day average, with the day's low right at or just below the 10-day average (at most 1% above it) and the close back above the 10-day.
▲G Gap-up buy point — opens above the prior day's high; the gap from the prior close is at least 0.75× the average true range of the previous 40 sessions; volume is at least 1.5× its 50-day average; and the day's range comes within 5% of the base high. Stop = the gap day's low.
▲B Breakout — the first close above the base high (the highest high of the prior 6 months, set at least 3 weeks earlier) on volume at least 1.5× its 50-day average — the book's 50%-above-average breakout rule.
△ Not a buy — a pocket pivot or gap that breaks one of the book's rules — extended (low more than 1% above the 10-day), closed below the 10-day, below the 50-day, a downtrend (50-day falling, under the 200-day), the lower third of the base, wedging up on lighter volume, a V-shaped drop to the 20-day that never touched the 50-day, a wide and loose base (deeper than 50% or weekly swings of 10%+), or a gap extended more than 5% above the base high or still inside the base. Shown for the last 60 sessions only.
The flags are judged on SIMPLE moving averages, as in the book's charts: the 10-day (location, extended) and 50-day (above it), plus the 20-day (V-shape) and 200-day (downtrend). Every chart shows the 10- and 50-day lines — dotted where the page's own lines are EMAs.
Hover a marker to see its numbers, whether the day closed in the upper half of its range (the book calls a mid-range close weak) and whether trading was quiet before it. The thresholds freeze a discretionary book (Trade Like an O'Neil Disciple, ch. 5) into rules: reading aids, not tested signals. On NSE only the breakout-day versions are measured — gap-ups helped (42.3% vs 34.5% reached +20% in 60 days); pocket-pivot volume barely did, because 77% of breakouts have it.

The eight gates
1
Composite Rating ≥ 90 (1–99) A master filter that distils every key measure into one score. 90+ keeps only the top 10% of the market on combined technical and fundamental strength.
2
EPS Strength ≥ 80 (1–99) A highly rated track record of strong and accelerating current and annual earnings growth.
3
Price Strength (RS Rating) ≥ 80 (1–99) Clear price leadership over the past year — a percentile rank against every other stock in India. Prevents buying laggards just because they look cheap.
4
SMR Rating ≥ 80 (1–99) Sales growth, profit margins and Return on Equity combined. 80+ puts the business in the top 20% of the Indian market — it looks underneath the share price at the quality of the company itself.
5
Buyer Demand (A/D Rating) A or B Reads the footprints of big money — confirms large, persistent institutional demand is funding the advance. A/B = accumulation, C is neutral, D/E = distribution.
6
Group Rank ≤ 80 (of 180) ⚠️ The "197 groups" figure usually quoted for this rule is IBD's US count. MarketSmith India ranks 180 groups, 0 (strongest) to 179 — verified on the live universe. So ≤36 is the true top fifth, ≤40 is the top 22.8%, and the current ≤80 keeps the strongest 44%. O'Neil's claim is that roughly HALF a stock's move comes from its industry group, which is why the gate exists at all.
7
Distance to pivot -15% to +5% Sign convention: positive means the stock is ABOVE its pivot; negative means it is still below and has yet to reach the buy point. So the band runs from 15% below the buy point to 5% above it. This is the gate that makes the screen actionable rather than merely a quality list — measured on 2026-09-08 it cut the ratings survivors from 46 to 9, because 40 of those 46 were already a median +23% past their buy point and unbuyable under either O'Neil's or Minervini's rules. The upper bound is Minervini's own 5% limit (raised from 3% on 2026-09-08); the lower bound keeps forming bases on the list. Two source columns are unified here: forming bases carry "% to pivot", completed bases carry "% from pivot", and they are mutually exclusive.
8
Liquidity — turnover ≥ ₹2 Cr/day, and not flagged Low Liquidity A stock can pass every quality gate and still be untradeable. Turnover is average daily volume × price. Where download_flags.py has run for the date, MarketSmith's own Low Liquidity red flag (the one on its Surveillance tab) is applied on top — that flag is authoritative, the turnover figure is the always-available proxy. A missing flag file is treated as "unknown", never as "liquid".

Scoring logic
All six criteria are hard gates — a stock either passes or is excluded. The score only orders the survivors, so a high score can never rescue a failed gate.
Weighted blend, each component scaled 0–100:

What this score is not. It is a reading order, not evidence of edge. The weights are judgement, not the output of a backtest — no historical validation has been run on them, so do not treat a higher score as a higher expected return.

Composite is partly redundant here. It is a blend of the other five, and all five are already gated individually — on the 2026-08-08 universe, removing the Composite gate changed the result by zero names. It is kept because it is the screen as specified, but it is not doing the work.

There is no chart criterion in this screen. No base structure, no pivot, no distance from a buy point, no volume confirmation. It is a quality pre-filter that decides what earns your attention — the base, the pivot, the market regime and the position size remain your work. A rating is a starting line, not a finish line.
