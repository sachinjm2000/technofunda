Why five numbers instead of one. A single RS reading cannot tell a leader from a bounce: the most beaten-down stock in the market posts the biggest percentage gain in a relief rally, precisely because it fell hardest. Durable leadership agrees with itself across time — a bounce is a short-horizon spike sitting on a weak long-term record. Read the row across.
TRUE LEADER — top decile on every horizon. Strong now AND strong for a year.
STAGE 2 CONFIRMED — 6M and 1Y lead, 1M cooler. A normal pullback inside a running trend, not a top.
EMERGING — short horizons lead, the year has not confirmed yet. Could become either of the other two.
BOUNCE — 1M leads while 1Y is weak. A relief rally inside a downtrend. This is the configuration a single-number RS screen cannot see, and the reason this tab exists.
The absolute-trend guard is doing real work here. RS is relative: in a broad decline a stock ranks 95 by falling LESS than everything else while being in absolute Stage 4. Every row above is also above a rising 200DMA, which is the standard fix. Without that filter this board would surface names that are merely losing more slowly.
RS_Classic is IBD's published composite — 0.4×ROC(63) + 0.2×ROC(126) + 0.2×ROC(189) + 0.2×ROC(252) — so 40% of the weight sits on the most recent quarter, by design, to reward acceleration.

How this board is computed

Universe. Every symbol in our price cache with ≥256 bars that can be scored on all five lenses, percentile-ranked 1–99 against each other. Consensus. Top decile (≥90) on ≥3 of 4 lenses — 3M, 6M, 1Y and the IBD Classic composite. 1M is shown but does not count. Guards. Above a rising 200DMA; turnover ≥ ₹1 Cr/day; base stage 1–2; T2T vetoed. Percentiles are against OUR cache, not MarketSmith's universe — the MSI RS column is theirs, shown alongside so a divergence is visible. Computed once per session from the last completed close.
